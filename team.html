Team Meeting: Getting on One Accord

A 30-minute calibration activity for a Sunnyside grade-level team, PLC, or staff meeting. The team works through real Sunnyside scenarios, votes, sees the matrix answer, and talks it through until everyone would respond the same way. Consistent responses across classrooms are how we make discipline fair for every student.

Agenda (30 minutes)

  1. Why we calibrate (5 min). Share the research on the right. Set the norm: we are calibrating a system, not judging anyone's classroom.
  2. Scenarios (20 min). For each one, everyone decides silently, then votes by show of hands. Tally with the + buttons, reveal the answer, and use the discussion question. Flag anything the team wants to revisit.
  3. Team agreements (5 min). Edit and print the agreements below, and have everyone sign.

Facilitator keys: → next, ← back, R reveal, F full screen.

Why calibration matters

  • Racial gaps in discipline are largest for subjective referrals like "defiance" and "disrespect," and at predictable decision points (Smolkowski et al., 2016; Girvan, Gion, McIntosh, & Smolkowski, 2017).
  • In one district, the 5% of teachers who referred most often doubled the racial gap in referrals (Liu, Penner, & Gao, 2023).
  • Shared definitions, clear procedures, and looking at data together are recommended strategies for fair discipline (McIntosh, Ellwood, McCall, & Girvan, 2018).
  • Our three problems (consequences without intervention, no proof of intervention, and mild behaviors logged as Discipline) are all consistency problems.
